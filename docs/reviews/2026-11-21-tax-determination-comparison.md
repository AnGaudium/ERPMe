## 2. NativeFlows today (as read, not changed)

- Key: `tax_matrix (direction, partner_tax_group_code, item_tax_group_code, effective_from) → tax_code_ids` (ADR-0041 rule 3; DS-05 `uq_tax_matrix_key`). Dated rates on `tax_rate.effective_from`, used rows never change (rule 2).
- Product axis: `item_tax_group_id` on `product`, empty → the category chain's `default_item_tax_group_id` → company default (DS-20 notes of 2026-11-21). Equivalent to Oracle's category-set default and better than Odoo's.
- Partner axis: `partner.partner_tax_group_id`; the document uses the bill-to party's group (else sold-to) on sales and the invoicing party's on purchases (ADR-0074 rule 4). ADR-0018 rule 16 says "each document where it differs" has a group, but I found **no document-header column** for it in the DS DDL (grep of `partner_tax_group` hits master data and the matrix only).
- Transaction axis: **none of its own**. In DS-19 the transaction type is encoded as partner groups: `domestic`, `export` (sales), `no_vat_invoice`, `foreign_supplier` (purchases). The ship-to party and address exist on documents (ADR-0074 rules 1–3; ADR-0068, ADR-0074 note on the delivery address), but **no tax rule reads them**; Incoterms are advisory and must not be read by tax (ADR-0068).
- Override: a line may differ from the matrix only as ADR-0018 rule 16 allows (interim: lines taken from an external document), shown as `tax_code_differs_from_matrix`. Snapshot: `document_line_tax` rows with rate, `tax_rate_id` and the matrix row used (rule 6). Corrections take the original line's tax date, codes and rates (rule 8). Import codes come from the customs declaration, not the matrix.

## 3. Common pattern

- **Two inputs always exist**: a product tax category on the product (or its category) and something that says what kind of transaction this is. Every system keeps the product side on the master and copies it to the line, where it can be changed (SAP item TAXM1, D365 line item group, Oracle line product fiscal classification, Odoo line taxes).
- **The transaction side is never only a partner attribute.** SAP derives it from departure country (plant) and destination country (ship-to) *plus* the customer classification; D365 lets rules derive the "sales tax group" from business process and ship-from / ship-to, the customer's group being only the default; Oracle separates party, product, process and place factors; Odoo and NetSuite pick the delivery / ship-to address. Only legacy D365 and B1-by-BP rely on a partner value alone, and both added address- or rule-based paths.
- **Defaults then rules then override**: master-data default → rule-based determination at line creation / recalculation → explicit user override (D365 checkbox, SAP header countries, Oracle controlled override). The override is visible and, in D365 and Oracle, switchable per partner or per tax.
- **Most-specific or first-in-priority wins**: SAP access sequence order, D365 column weight, Oracle rule priority, Odoo ranked tuple, B1 priority list. Nobody uses "all must match" except D365's final intersection of two code sets.
- **Dated rates on the code**, not on the mapping, in D365, Oracle and NetSuite; SAP dates condition records; Odoo has none. The tax date is a separate, configurable choice (D365 packing-slip date; NativeFlows `tax.rate_date_basis`).
- **Differences**: what is the unit of the result (SAP and B1 return one tax code; D365 and NativeFlows return a set; Odoo maps a set to a set); whether the destination party is ship-to or payer / bill-to (SAP makes it a setting; Odoo uses delivery with an EU exception); whether goods and services use different locations (Oracle has explicit place-of-supply rules, the others leave it to configuration).

## 4. Cases a two-axis matrix (partner group × item group) cannot express

Checked against the systems above; "NF" = the current NativeFlows design with DS-19 groups.

1. **Foreign customer, domestic delivery.** The bill-to is abroad, goods stay in the country. SAP: destination = ship-to country → domestic code. Odoo: delivery address → domestic position. NF: bill-to group `export` → 0%, which is the wrong treatment under the usual rule that goods leaving the country define an export (legal basis is the author's; flagged).
2. **Domestic customer, delivery abroad** (the customer exports through us, or we ship to its foreign site). SAP and Odoo follow the ship-to country; NF gives `domestic`. Whether 0% applies is a legal rule (customs evidence), but the matrix cannot even propose it.
3. **One partner, both cases.** A customer with domestic and foreign ship-to sites, or a buyer located in an export-processing zone with a second site outside it: one partner group cannot be right for both. Odoo handles this with a fiscal position per delivery contact or zip / state range; SAP with destination country (same-country zones need a region / classification, W); D365 with ship-to columns in applicability rules.
4. **Export-processing zone inside the country.** Same country on both sides, so country-based logic (SAP, NetSuite) does not see it either; it needs a flag on the ship-to address or site (Odoo zip range or a dedicated position; Oracle a party-site fiscal classification). NF has no address-level tax attribute.
5. **Drop shipment and plants abroad.** Ship-from is the supplier's or a foreign plant's country, not the company's. SAP's departure country comes from the delivering plant; D365 has a ship-from column; NF has no ship-from in the key.
6. **Triangular trade / chain transactions** (EU-style simplification, or a sale invoiced to one party and shipped from a third country). SAP has dedicated indicators (W); Oracle uses place-of-supply rules; a pairwise matrix has nowhere to put a third location.
7. **Services where place of supply differs from ship-to.** Services usually follow the customer's establishment (bill-to) or performance location, not a delivery address; goods follow movement. Oracle models this explicitly (place-of-supply rule by product type); D365 can add item code / business process columns; NF's single partner group applies the same treatment to goods and service lines on one document.
8. **Mixed document.** One order with goods shipped abroad and a domestic service: the treatment must be per line. D365 and Oracle determine per line; NF determines per document partner group.
9. **Purchases: foreign supplier delivering from domestic stock**, or a domestic supplier without e-invoice for one purchase only. `foreign_supplier` / `no_vat_invoice` as a partner group fixes the treatment for all purchases from that supplier. SAP proposes the purchase tax code per item and lets the user change it; NF's interim rule 16 allows differences only for lines from external documents.
10. **Partner status changes over time** (registration gained or lost, exemption certificate expiring). The matrix is dated, the partner group is not; only the document snapshot protects history. D365 and Oracle derive at transaction time and store the result on the line.
11. **Prepayments / advance invoices.** Tax date and rate come from the advance and are deducted on the final invoice; this is a date and linkage rule, not a matrix key. No system above solves it in the determination table; NF needs it in the correction / settlement rules (unverified whether DS covers it).
12. **Returns and credit notes.** Must inherit the original line's treatment, codes and rate, not re-determine (SAP copies conditions from the reference document, W; Odoo copies line taxes; NF rule 8 already does this). A two-axis re-lookup would be wrong after a partner group or matrix change, so inheritance by copy is the correct design and NF has it.

Cases 1–9 share one cause: the transaction type is stored on the partner instead of being decided per document (or line) from the ship-to, ship-from, product kind and partner status.

## 5. Options for NativeFlows

**Option A: document-level partner group with an override (Odoo-like, legacy D365).**
- Key unchanged: (direction, partner tax group, item tax group, tax date).
- Add `partner_tax_group_id` on the document header (snapshot), defaulted from the bill-to / invoicing party (ADR-0074 rule 4); the user may change it on the document; optionally a line-level group for mixed documents.
- Overrides: header change re-proposes all lines; check result `partner_tax_group_differs_from_partner`.
- Cost for a small coding model: very low (one column, one default, one check). Fixes case 3 only by manual action; cases 1, 2, 4–8 depend on the user remembering.

**Option B (recommended): derived transaction treatment on the document, matrix keeps two axes (D365 Tax Calculation-like, with SAP's country logic).**
- Split today's partner tax group into two concepts:
  - `partner_tax_status` on the partner (what the party *is*: e.g. registered domestic, not registered / no VAT invoice, foreign, exempt with certificate); not a matrix key.
  - `tax_treatment` on the document header and each line (what the transaction *is*: `domestic`, `export_goods`, `export_zone`, `foreign_service`, `reverse_charge`, `import`, `no_vat_invoice`, … as package codes). This is the matrix's first axis, so `tax_matrix (direction, tax_treatment, item_tax_group, effective_from)` keeps today's shape and DS-19 rows map one to one (`domestic`, `export`, `no_vat_invoice`, `foreign_supplier` are already treatments in disguise).
- Derivation: package rule table `tax_treatment_rule` (direction, document type, ship-from country, ship-to country, ship-to `is_tax_zone` flag on `partner_address` / `site`, partner tax status, item kind goods / service, priority, `effective_from`, → `tax_treatment`). Empty column = any; most filled columns wins, ties by `priority` (D365's weight rule; easy to test). Ship-from = the document's site / warehouse country; ship-to = the ship-to address (ADR-0074), else bill-to; for services the package may set "use bill-to" (place-of-supply setting below).
- Defaulting and snapshot: `Create` / `Update` derive the header treatment, lines inherit it, service lines may get their own; store `tax_treatment` and `tax_treatment_source` (`rule:<id>` or `manual`) on header and line, frozen with the content like `document_line_tax`. Corrections copy the original line's treatment (extends rule 8).
- Override: user may set the header or line treatment explicitly → check result `tax_treatment_differs_from_rule` (as `tax_code_differs_from_matrix` today); the tax-code-level override of rule 16 stays separate and rarer.
- Cost: moderate and mechanical: one rule table with a deterministic "most specific wins" function, two columns per header / line, one address flag, a renamed CSV column. A small model can implement and test it from a table of examples (EC-style); no expression language.

**Option C: full rule engine (Oracle Fusion / SAP condition technique).**
- Ordered rule types (applicability, place of supply, status, rate) over arbitrary determining factors, or access sequences over configurable condition tables.
- Covers cases 6 and 7 natively. Cost: high; configuration becomes a program, hard for a small model to implement correctly and for customers to verify; violates the lean first release (D5). Keep as the extension path, not Phase 1.

**Recommendation: Option B.** Reasons: (1) it moves the transaction type off the partner, which is the single cause of cases 1–9, and matches what every surveyed system does; (2) the matrix, its verification gate, company rows and DS-19 content keep their shape, so ADR-0041 rules 2–9 survive with a rename; (3) the derivation is one table with D365's documented weight rule, which a small model can implement and the lab can test exhaustively; (4) the snapshot on header and line keeps history correct when partner status or rules change (case 10) and fits rule 8 for corrections (case 12); (5) it does not read Incoterms, keeping ADR-0068's advisory rule. It needs an ADR amending ADR-0041 rule 3 and 7, ADR-0018 rule 16 and ADR-0074 rule 4, and the DS-19 treatment list with its legal basis is the author's (reserved: tax rules).

**Settings, because the systems genuinely differ** (package defaults, company may choose where the law allows):
- `tax.destination_party` per direction: ship-to, bill-to or payer (SAP's sales-organization rule; Odoo's delivery-with-exception).
- `tax.place_of_supply_services`: bill-to establishment or ship-to / performance location (Oracle's place-of-supply rule).
- `tax.treatment_override`: who may override header / line treatment and whether a reason is required (D365 per partner/document; Oracle per tax).
- `tax.rederive_on_change`: re-derive the treatment when ship-to, site or partner changes before freezing (SAP re-determines on header change; D365 on recalculation).
- Existing `tax.rate_date_basis` stays; the treatment rule table is dated like the matrix.

## 6. What I could not verify

- SAP: official S/4HANA on-premise help for the standard MWST access sequence tables (only KBAs and training); whether changing the departure country re-determines VBAP-TAXM1 (two KBAs conflict); SAP's triangular-deal and same-country-zone handling; how credit memos copy tax conditions (from knowledge, W).
- D365: the legacy defaulting of the sales tax group from the delivery address to header and line (community material only); the current Finance page for tax code values with from / to dates (AX 2012 page used).
- Oracle: the exact defaulting and override of intended use and transaction business category on a line; the processing order of applicability vs place-of-supply rules differs between documentation versions.
- Odoo: that product categories carry no taxes and that rate changes need a new tax record (not checked in source today); NetSuite hierarchy and dated rates; Business One rate history by effective date.
- Legal content (out of scope and the author's): whether goods delivered abroad for a domestic customer, goods sold to a foreign customer but delivered domestically, or sales into a Vietnamese export-processing zone are 0%, and on what evidence.
