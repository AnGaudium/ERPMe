# ADR-0061: Barcodes

Status: accepted (2026-11-15) under ADR-0005, reviewed by Claude Fable 5.2 (builder Claude Opus 5.5) after four review rounds. Design items: DS-20 (the barcode registry), DS-42 (scanning in warehouse steps), DS-27 (labels), DS-31 (PWA capture). Related decisions: D2, D3, D5, D14. Principles 12, 13. ADR-0002, ADR-0004, ADR-0006, ADR-0008, ADR-0009, ADR-0016, ADR-0018, ADR-0020, ADR-0023, ADR-0029, ADR-0040, ADR-0044, ADR-0045, ADR-0049, ADR-0051, ADR-0053, ADR-0054, ADR-0056.

## Decision

The author asked for barcode capability. The design already had barcodes per packaging unit (`item_barcode`), bin and handling-unit barcodes, scans as idempotent commands, bin and handling-unit labels, and a PWA that scans with the browser's BarcodeDetector. It lacked a standard parser, check digits, a barcode type, labels for items, lots and serials, a symbology choice, and one query that says what a scanned string is. This ADR adds them. It follows the 2026-11-15 survey of GS1 standards and of how SAP EWM, Dynamics 365, Odoo, NetSuite and Business Central handle scanning.

**One GS1 parser.** The server parses GS1 element strings and GS1 Digital Link URIs with an Application Identifier table generated from the GS1 Barcode Syntax Dictionary, pinned to one version. It checks check digits, dates, character sets, variable measures and the pairs of AIs that must or must not appear together. The PWA runs the GS1 Syntax Engine in WebAssembly for instant feedback, and one test corpus keeps the two in agreement. The server is the authority.

**One registry.** Every barcode of a company is a row of `barcode`, unique per company across types: item unit, bin, handling unit, lot, serial, document, and reserved codes. The typed tables keep their own barcodes. A trigger registers each one, so the database refuses a bin code that equals an item code, also under concurrent inserts. A retired barcode stays reserved, so an old label never resolves to another object, but it may return to its own owner. Labels of an old system stay readable as legacy codes.

**One query.** `EvaluateBarcode` reads the symbology identifier, then GS1 keys, then the registry, then the step's context, and returns typed candidates or a typed issue, never a guess. Posting steps confirm a scan against what they expect; search only opens records.

**Capture and labels.** A receipt scan of a supplier's GS1 label fills item, unit, lot, expiry, serial and quantity in the command's input, and a variable measure enters in its own unit and converts as ADR-0056 says. Labels for bins, handling units, items, lots, serials and documents render as PDF through the print engine of ADR-0049, with Code 128, GS1-128 and GS1 DataMatrix drawn as vectors.

**Country-neutral core.** GS1 is a global standard and nothing in the core depends on one country. A country's traceability codes or digital product passports plug into a generic package hook; the Vietnamese one is flagged for `l10n_vn` (ADR-0053).

ZPL and a print agent, GS1 location and asset keys, Digital Link generation, an offline queue and SSCC assignment come after Phase 1.

### Rules

**The GS1 parser**

1. **One parser and one table.** `erpme.gs1` is a pure library of the core (no I/O, no clock: the business date is a parameter), used by every command and query that reads a scan.
   - **The AI table** is generated by `erpme-dev generate` from the GS1 Barcode Syntax Dictionary file, vendored at a pinned release with its version, into `backend/generated/gs1/`. Nobody edits it by hand. Each entry gives the AI, its title, its components (numeric `N` or alphanumeric `X`, fixed or variable length, minimum and maximum), whether it has a predefined length, its linters (check digit, date, character set, decimal position), and the AIs it requires or excludes.
   - **The version** (`gs1_syntax_dictionary_version`) is part of every parse result, so a stored result names the table it used. A new Dictionary release is a pull request that regenerates the table and runs the corpus; it is reviewed once or twice a year.
   - **The generator fails** when the Dictionary breaks an assumption of this ADR: an AI whose length does not follow from its first two digits, or a predefined-length prefix whose entries disagree on length.
   - **One test corpus**, GS1's syntax tests plus ERPMe's cases, runs in CI against the server parser and against the PWA's GS1 Syntax Engine (WebAssembly, Apache-2.0), pinned to a release that embeds the same Dictionary version. The PWA's result is feedback only; the server parses the raw scan again and decides (rule 16).
   - **Unverified.** The predefined-length list of rule 2 and the AI pairs named in rule 3 come from the 2026-11-15 survey and from GS1 documents read there, not from the current Dictionary; the lab uses a hand-written subset. The generated table and the corpus decide, and the open questions carry the check.

2. **Element strings.** A GS1 barcode carries element strings: an AI of two to four digits followed by its data.
   - **AI length.** The first two digits of an AI give its length; the parser reads that many digits and looks the AI up. An AI the table does not hold is `gs1_invalid` with reason `ai_unknown`.
   - **Predefined length.** AIs whose first two digits are 00 (20 characters in total, AI included), 01, 02, 03 (16), 04 (18), 11 to 19 (8), 20 (4), 31 to 36 (10) and 41 (16) have a predefined length; the parser reads exactly that many characters and needs no separator. Every other AI is variable or not predefined, and ends at the separator or at the end of the data. The generated table, not this list, is authoritative.
   - **Separators.** FNC1 in the first position marks a GS1 symbol; scanners transmit it as the symbology identifier. FNC1 after an element is transmitted as GS (ASCII 29). A leading GS is dropped. A GS after a predefined-length element is tolerated. A variable-length element that is not last must end with GS; data longer than the AI's maximum is `gs1_invalid` with reason `length`.
   - **Transport.** A keyboard wedge often cannot send ASCII 29, so the scanner sends a substitute character, which the company setting `barcode.gs_substitute` (default `~`) names. The parser maps it back to GS only in data classified as an element string (rule 13), never in a Digital Link URI, where `~` is a legal character, nor in internal codes, whose format excludes it; GS1 character set 82 excludes it too. One trailing CR, LF, CR LF or Tab is the scanner's suffix and is removed. Any other control character is `scan_malformed`.
   - **Parentheses** are human-readable only. Data that contains `(01)` style parentheses is not an element string. Manual entry may use the human-readable form; the screen converts it before the command, and the command records `source: manual`.
   - **Raw length.** A raw scan holds at most 512 characters.

3. **Validation.** After splitting, the parser checks every element against the table and reports the first failure per element as `gs1_invalid` with `details.reason`:
   - `format`: an `N` component holds a non-digit, or an `X` component holds a character outside GS1 character set 82 (letters A–Z and a–z, digits, and `! " % & ' ( ) * + , - . / : ; < = > ? _`), or outside character set 39 where the table says so;
   - `length`: a fixed component of the wrong length, or a variable one outside its bounds;
   - `check_digit`: a key with a wrong check digit (rule 4);
   - `date`: an impossible date, or day 00 where the AI does not allow it (rule 5);
   - `pair_required`: an AI without one of the AIs the table requires with it, for example (21) without (01) or (8006), or (37) without (02) or (8026);
   - `pair_forbidden`: two AIs the table excludes together, for example (01) and (02);
   - `repeated_differs`: one AI twice with different values (twice with the same value is accepted once);
   - `separator_suspect`: the last element is variable-length and its value ends with characters that themselves parse completely as one or more valid predefined-length elements, at least a date or a key with a valid check digit (`LOT1717261130` as lot `LOT1` followed by (17) 261130). That is the trace of a wedge that dropped the GS. It is not raised when the transport is proven (the scan holds a GS after its first element, or came from the WebAssembly decoder, which reports FNC1 itself, `scan.decoder: wasm`), nor when the whole value is an existing lot or serial of the scanned item or the one the step expects. Otherwise the scan stops, and the screen lets the person confirm the value as read (`scan.confirmed_as_read: true` in the next call) instead of typing it.

   Note ([ADR-0063](0063-configurable-policy-settings.md), proposed 2026-11-15): the stop is the setting `barcode.separator_suspect` per scanner profile: `stop_and_confirm` (default) or `trust_transport` for a profile proven to send GS (rule 26).

4. **Check digits.** GTIN-8, GTIN-12, GTIN-13, GTIN-14, SSCC, GLN and GDTI use one rule: weight the data digits 3, 1, 3, 1, … from the rightmost data digit leftward; the check digit is (10 − sum mod 10) mod 10.
   - **Worked example** with a fictional company prefix `8931234` (the GS1 prefix 893 followed by an invented company number; the example names no real company): item reference `56789` gives the data digits `893123456789`. From the right: 9×3 + 8×1 + 7×3 + 6×1 + 5×3 + 4×1 + 3×3 + 2×1 + 1×3 + 3×1 + 9×3 + 8×1 = 133; (10 − 3) mod 10 = 7; the GTIN-13 is `8931234567897`.
   - **Stored as 14 digits.** Every GTIN is stored and compared as 14 digits, left-padded with zeros: `08931234567897`. (01) always carries 14 digits. The symbology a scan came in is kept in the parse result, never in the key.
   - **A case of the same item** with indicator digit 1 has the data digits `1893123456789` and check digit 4: `18931234567894`. The check digit changes with the indicator, so it is computed, never copied. The packaging level a GTIN stands for comes from `item_barcode`'s unit, never from the indicator digit alone; indicator 9 marks a variable-measure trade item.
   - **SSCC.** 18 digits: extension digit, company prefix, serial reference, check digit. With extension 3, the fictional prefix and serial reference `000000000`: `389312340000000005`.
   - **No origin from a prefix.** A GS1 prefix tells which GS1 member organization issued the company prefix, not where the goods were made. No rule derives a country of origin, a tax treatment or a supplier from a GTIN or SSCC prefix; origin is the product's `country_of_origin` (ADR-0021 rule 13).

5. **Dates and measures.**
   - **Dates** (AIs 11, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17 and the date part of others) are YYMMDD. The century follows the GS1 sliding window against the business date of the step (never the wall clock, so a replay gives the same date): with D = YY − (business year mod 100), D ≥ 51 means the previous century, D ≤ −50 the next century, otherwise the current one. In 2026, `76` is 2076 and `77` is 1977.
   - **Day 00** is accepted only where the table's date linter allows it. ERPMe reads it as the last day of the month for (12) due, (15) best before, (16) sell by and (17) expiry, and as the first day for (11) production and (13) packaging, and records `day_precision: month` in the element.
   - **Variable measures** (310n to 369n): the fourth digit n is the number of decimals, 0 to 5, so `3102001156` is 11.56. The unit follows the AI. Phase 1 maps the AIs whose unit is a core unit (ADR-0056 rule 2): 310n net weight to `KGM`, 311n length to `MTR`, 314n area to `MTK`, 315n net volume to `LTR`, 316n net volume to `MTQ`. Other measures, for example 320n in pounds or 330n gross weight of a logistic unit, stay in the parse result and are reported as `gs1_element_ignored`; they never become a quantity.
   - Counts (30) and (37) are integers.

   Note ([ADR-0063](0063-configurable-policy-settings.md), proposed 2026-11-15): the day 00 readings are the settings `barcode.gs1.day00_policy.<ai>` per company and category (`last_day`, `first_day` or `reject`; defaults as here); unknown AIs follow `barcode.unknown_ai_policy` (rule 26).

6. **GS1 Digital Link.** A URI `https://{domain}/{optional path}/01/{gtin}[/22/{cpv}][/10/{lot}][/21/{serial}][?17=YYMMDD&3103=000500]` is another encoding of element strings.
   - **Parsing.** The primary key is the rightmost path segment that is a primary key AI of the table (01, 00, 414, 8003, 8004, 8006 and the others the Dictionary marks) and is followed only by its qualifiers, in the order the table gives, so a custom path such as `/00/01/{gtin}` reads as (01). The GS1 Syntax Engine is believed to search the same way; the shared corpus confirms it (unverified). The query string adds data attributes whose keys are numeric AIs. Segments are percent-decoded. A GTIN of 8, 12 or 13 digits is padded to 14. Query keys that are not AIs are ignored. Alphabetic short names and compressed Digital Link URIs are not read in Phase 1.
   - **Same validation.** The elements go through rules 3 to 5 like an element string.
   - **Never fetched.** The parser never opens the URI: no network on the scan path, and no following of a link printed by someone else. The domain is kept in the result as untrusted text and plays no part in resolution.

**The registry**

7. **The barcode registry.** `barcode` (DS-20, module `master_data`) has one row per barcode of a company: `company_id`, `barcode` (normalized), `barcode_type`, `encoding` (`gtin`, `sscc` or `internal`), `is_legacy` (rule 8), `retired_type`, `retired_owner_id`, `retired_at`, `recorded_at`; key (`company_id`, `barcode`).
   - **Types.** `item_unit` (an `item_barcode` row: item and unit), `bin`, `handling_unit`, `lot`, `serial`, `document` (rule 9), and `internal`: a reserved code with no owner, either retired from another type (`retired_type` and `retired_owner_id` set) or reserved by the company (`CreateBarcodeReservation`, for labels of an old system still in circulation or badges a later feature reads).
   - **Typed tables keep their barcodes.** `item_barcode.barcode` (with its unit, which ADR-0056 proposes on a line), `bin.barcode`, `handling_unit.barcode`, and new nullable `lot.barcode` and `serial_number.barcode` for internal labels. Each typed table keeps its own uniqueness. The registry adds uniqueness across types.
   - **Why a shared uniqueness table and not one table of all barcodes.** One table would replace `item_barcode`, `bin.barcode` and `handling_unit.barcode`, rewrite three accepted ADRs, and lose the unit binding and the surrogate key that ADR-0056 gives `item_barcode`. Typed tables plus one registry keep every accepted shape, put the cross-type rule in keys the database holds, and give the query one lookup.
   - **Written by a trigger.** `barcode_register`, an AFTER trigger on each typed table, inserts the registry row when a barcode is set, so no command can forget it. It refuses a value that is not in normalized form (`barcode_not_normalized`) and a GTIN or SSCC with a wrong check digit. The registry is derived from the typed rows, so it has no surrogate ID. Commands insert only reserved rows (`CreateBarcodeReservation`) and delete only reserved or retired rows (`DeleteBarcodeReservation`).
   - **Two keys, both held under concurrency.** The primary key keeps one row per code. The unique index `ux_barcode_numeric_padded` on (`company_id`, the code padded to 14 digits) over every code of 1 to 14 digits keeps a GTIN and a numeric legacy code, or two numeric legacy codes, from meaning the same digits (`08931234567897` and legacy `8931234567897`; legacy `0123456789012` and `123456789012`). Two transactions that insert colliding codes at once cannot both commit: the second waits for the first and fails. The trigger maps both violations to `barcode_taken`.
   - **Normalized form.** A GTIN is 14 digits, an SSCC 18 digits, an internal code upper case, a legacy code upper case and never padded (rule 8). Commands store the normalized form in the typed table; scans are normalized the same way before the lookup, so `a-03-02` typed on a wedge with caps lock finds `A-03-02`.
   - **Allowed encodings.** `item_unit`: a GTIN or an internal code. `handling_unit`: an SSCC or an internal code. `bin`, `lot`, `serial`, `document`: an internal code. `internal`: any.
   - **Retirement.** When a typed row is deleted or its barcode changes, the trigger turns the old registry row into `internal` with `retired_type`, `retired_owner_id` and `retired_at`. The owner is the item for an item barcode and the row itself for the other types. A retired code is never given to another object, so a stale label on a rack or a carton resolves to `barcode_retired`, never to a new owner. It may return to its own owner: when the same type and owner set the code again (a bin changed back from `R-01X` to `R-01`, a barcode deleted and re-created on the same item), the trigger revives the row.
   - **Deleting reservations.** `DeleteBarcodeReservation` (`human_only`, declared minimum level `full`, with a reason) deletes one reserved or retired row, when the company knows that no label with that code survives. For a retired SSCC it waits until `barcode.sscc_reuse_months` have passed since retirement (`barcode_reuse_period_running`). GS1 sets a minimum period before an SSCC may be reused; the period is a GS1 rule, the same in every country, so it is a company setting like the other `barcode.*` settings, default 12 months, unverified against the current GS1 General Specifications (open question).
   - **Supplier SSCCs come back by themselves.** A daily system job, `RunRetiredSsccCleanup` (registered `is_internal_only`, so no role holds it and it has no REST endpoint or tool; ADR-0054), deletes every retired SSCC row whose period has passed and whose company prefix is not one of the company's own prefixes, active or not (rule 11): a supplier's pallet label may then be received again without a person. An SSCC under the company's own prefixes, which ERPMe generated, is deleted only by a person with `DeleteBarcodeReservation`, and ERPMe never reissues one itself (rule 25).

8. **Internal codes.** Every barcode that is not a GS1 key is an internal code:
   - **Format.** One upper-case ASCII letter, then up to 47 characters from `A`–`Z`, `0`–`9`, `-`, `.`, `/` and `_` (`barcode_format_invalid`). A leading letter keeps internal codes apart from GTINs, SSCCs and element strings, which start with a digit, and from Digital Link URIs. Upper case only, because wedges and keyboard layouts flip case. ASCII only, because Code 128 and GS1 character set 82 carry nothing else; text in any language belongs on the human-readable line of a label (rule 21).
   - **Generated codes** take a type prefix from company settings: `barcode.prefix.handling_unit` (`HU-`), `barcode.prefix.lot` (`L-`), `barcode.prefix.serial` (`SN-`), `barcode.prefix.item_unit` (`I-`), followed by a counter taken through the numbering lock helper (ADR-0009 rule 15). A bin's barcode defaults to its code (ADR-0023 rule 2) when the code fits the format; otherwise the person gives one.
   - **Lot and serial codes** are kept as they come: the database refuses only an empty code and puts no character rule on them, so migration and suppliers' paper lot numbers (`LOT 2026/11`, codes longer than 20 characters) are stored unchanged. A lot or serial code is unique per item (ADR-0020 rule 17), not per company, so it is not a barcode. The label rule of rule 21 decides how it is printed: a code of 1 to 20 characters of GS1 character set 82 goes into (10) or (21); any other code is printed through the lot's or serial's own internal barcode, which the command that creates such a lot or serial generates.
   - **Legacy codes.** Labels of an old system stay readable after migration (ADR-0044; ADR-0056 rule 21). `item_barcode`, `bin` and `handling_unit` gain `is_legacy_barcode` (default false), set when the row is created; the database refuses a change. Only an import batch or a person may set it (an agent's payload with it is refused with `permission_denied`). A legacy code:
     - is 1 to 48 characters of `A`–`Z`, `0`–`9`, `-`, `.`, `/` and `_`, upper case, starting with a letter or a digit, so old numeric codes are kept as they are; an old code with other characters (a space, `+`, `$`, `%` from the Code 39 set) is refused, and migration lists it for relabelling;
     - is registered with encoding `internal` and `is_legacy`, under its owner's type, and is unique in the registry like every other code; it is never padded, never parsed as GS1 and never treated as a GTIN;
     - when numeric, may not equal a registered GTIN or another numeric legacy code once both are padded to 14 digits (`barcode_taken`, held by `ux_barcode_numeric_padded`), so the fallbacks of rule 13 stay unambiguous;
     - gives the check result `legacy_code_gtin_like` when it is new, numeric, 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits long and passes a GTIN check digit: a scanner that reads it as EAN-13 or UPC-A still finds it (rule 13), but a supplier GTIN with the same digits can never be registered, and the company may prefer to relabel.

     New internal codes always follow the format above; a legacy code is only a way to keep existing labels.

9. **Document barcodes.** A document type may be registered `is_barcoded` (a registry flag of printable types, a note on ADR-0009 rule 15). Phase 1: purchase orders, goods receipts, sales orders, deliveries, transfer orders and production orders.
   - **The barcode is the document number.** `document.is_barcoded` is set from the flag at Create; `document_check_row_change` refuses any later change. When such a document is numbered, the trigger registers its number in upper case as type `document`.
   - **Reserved prefixes.** The number format of a barcoded type must start with `{prefix}` followed by a separator (`/`, `-` or `.`). That string (`PO/`) is reserved: no barcoded type's reserved string may start another's, and no barcode of another type may start with one (`barcode_prefix_reserved`, checked when a barcode is created and when a numbering configuration is created or changed).
   - **The whole number fits.** When a numbering configuration of a barcoded type is created or changed, and when a branch code or fiscal year code that such a format uses is created or changed, the command checks that every number the format can produce is an internal code: its literal characters and the codes it uses are in the internal character set (letters in either case, since the number is registered in upper case), and its longest number, with the counter at full width, has at most 48 characters (`numbering_barcode_invalid`, naming the part that fails). So numbering never fails on the registry's format check. The default format `{prefix}/{fiscal_year_code}/{counter:6}` (ADR-0009 rule 16) satisfies it when the codes do.

10. **Commands.** All `master_data_change` effect class unless stated, change-tracked, in batches (ADR-0008). Each uses a verb in its existing meaning (ADR-0004 rule 20):
    - `CreateItemBarcode` (item, unit, `barcode` or `generate`, optional `is_legacy_barcode`): the unit is the inventory unit or an active product unit of the item (`uom_not_allowed`, ADR-0056 rule 6). `generate: gtin13` takes the next item reference of the company's generation prefix (rule 11) and computes the check digit; `generate: gtin14` with an indicator digit 1 to 8 builds a case GTIN from a GTIN-13 of the same item (indicator, the GTIN-13's first 12 digits, a new check digit); `generate: internal` takes the item-unit prefix and counter. A GTIN maps to one item unit per company.
    - `UpdateItemBarcode` (item barcode, item, unit): corrects which item or unit a barcode belongs to, a supplier GTIN linked to the wrong item for example; the code and its registry row stay.
    - `DeleteItemBarcode` (item barcode): deletes the row; the code stays reserved for the item (rule 7).
    - `SetLotBarcode` and `SetSerialNumberBarcode` (the lot or serial, `barcode` or `generate: internal`): set its label code. No lot or serial update command exists to carry the barcode as a field, so they take the single-value form of Set, `Set<Record><Field>`, which a new glossary row describes. The form keeps the meaning of Set: it replaces, never merges (ADR-0004 rule 20). A handling unit gets its barcode when it is created (`PackHandlingUnit`, a receipt, a migration import): a generated internal code, the SSCC of a supplier's label (rule 17), or a legacy code. A bin changes its barcode with the bin's own update command (ADR-0023 rule 2), and takes a legacy code only when created by an import or a person.
    - `CreateBarcodeReservation` (barcode, reason) and `DeleteBarcodeReservation` (barcode, reason; `human_only`, declared minimum level `full`; rule 7).
    - `CreateGs1CompanyPrefix`, `UpdateGs1CompanyPrefix` (the generation flag and `is_active`) and `DeleteGs1CompanyPrefix` (rule 11).
    - `RunRetiredSsccCleanup`, the daily system job of rule 7, registered `is_internal_only` with leaf `product`, like `RunApprovalEscalation` (a note on ADR-0054 rule 24).
    - **Authorization leaves** (ADR-0054): `CreateItemBarcode`, `UpdateItemBarcode`, `DeleteItemBarcode`, `CreateBarcodeReservation`, `DeleteBarcodeReservation`, the three prefix commands and `ListItemLabel` under `product` (and `RunRetiredSsccCleanup`, which no level grants); `SetLotBarcode`, `SetSerialNumberBarcode`, `ListLotLabel` and `ListSerialLabel` under `warehouse`; `EvaluateBarcode` under `general_access`, which checks each candidate's own read permission (rule 12).
    - **Errors.** `barcode_taken` (any type in the company holds the code or its padded digits; `details` give the type and the owner's code when the caller may read it), `barcode_format_invalid`, `barcode_prefix_reserved`, `numbering_barcode_invalid`, `barcode_reuse_period_running`, `gs1_prefix_required`, `gs1_prefix_invalid`, `gs1_prefix_in_use`.

11. **Company GS1 settings.** GS1 is optional per company:
    - **Company prefixes** are rows of `gs1_company_prefix` (DS-20): `prefix` (4 to 12 digits), `is_active`, `is_generation_prefix`, `next_item_reference`. A company may hold several prefixes, since a short licence runs out (a nine-digit prefix gives 1,000 GTIN-13s), and at most one is the generation prefix, which must be active; the database holds both. Prefixes do not overlap (none starts another): an exclusion constraint on each prefix's range of 12-digit numbers holds it at every isolation level, and the command reports a violation as `gs1_prefix_invalid`, as it does a prefix of invalid length (the exact range is unverified). `generate: gtin13` needs a generation prefix with references left (`gs1_prefix_required`). An inactive prefix generates nothing new but stays the company's own, so the cleanup job of rule 7 never deletes an SSCC under it. `DeleteGs1CompanyPrefix` is refused (`gs1_prefix_in_use`) while any GTIN or SSCC of the registry, live or retired, lies under the prefix (its digits after the indicator or extension digit start with it); otherwise old prefixes are deactivated, not deleted. Resolution never depends on prefixes: every GTIN resolves through the registry.
    - `barcode.gs_substitute` (default `~`), the wedge's GS substitute (rule 2).
    - `barcode.prefix.<type>` (rule 8).
    - A company without a prefix works fully on internal codes and on the GTINs it receives from suppliers, which it registers with `CreateItemBarcode`.

**Resolution**

12. **`EvaluateBarcode`** is a registered query of module `inventory`, which reads master data and documents through their contracts. It is the third query form of ADR-0004 rule 21 (its note): it computes a result from the scan it is given and stores nothing. People, the PWA and agents call the same query.
    - **Input:** `scan` (`raw`, optional `symbology_identifier`, `source`: `wedge`, `camera` or `manual`, optional `decoder`: `native` or `wasm` for camera scans, optional `camera_format` as BarcodeDetector names it, optional `confirmed_as_read`), optional `context` (`mode`: `search` or `confirm`; `expected`: a list of `{type, id}` or `{type, scope}`, which may name the expected lot or serial code; `item_id` for lot and serial context; `warehouse_id`), and the business date.
    - **Output:** `normalized`, `symbology` (the identifier, or `none`), `encoding` (`gs1_element_string`, `digital_link`, `gtin`, `internal`), `elements` (AI to value, interpreted: ISO dates, decimal measures with their unit), `gs1_syntax_dictionary_version`, `candidates` (each `{type, id, code, item_id, uom_id, lot_id, lot_code, serial_number_id, serial_code, quantity, quantity_uom_id, expiry_date, match: exact | contextual}`) and `issues` (rule 14).
    - **Scope.** Candidates the caller may not read are left out; when every candidate is left out, the answer is `barcode_unknown`, so nothing leaks. `barcode_retired` names only the type the code had, never its old owner. Agents may call the query on their tool lists (ADR-0050), read-only.

13. **Resolution order.** One order, deterministic:
    1. **Symbology identifier** (sent by the scanner as `]` plus two characters, or by the WebAssembly decoder): `]C1`, `]e0`, `]d2`, `]Q3`, `]J1` mean a GS1 element string; `]E0` a GTIN-13 or a UPC-A sent as 13 digits with a leading zero, `]E4` a GTIN-8; `]I1` an ITF whose check character the scanner validated, a GTIN when it has 14 digits and plain data otherwise; `]Q1` or `]d1` with `http://` or `https://` a candidate Digital Link; `]C0`, `]A0`, `]Q1`, `]d1` otherwise plain data. Any identifier not listed (`]I0`, `]E3`, `]C2`, `]Q2`, `]L0`, …) means plain data, which goes to steps 4 and 5. A plain Code 128 that happens to start with `01` is never parsed as GS1 when the identifier arrives.
    2. **Without an identifier** (a wedge without the AIM prefix, a camera decoder that sends none): a scan that equals a registered legacy code, or whose digits padded to 14 equal a numeric legacy code, goes straight to step 4. Otherwise guarded rules apply, each reported as the check result `scan_heuristic_used`: a leading or embedded GS means an element string; 8, 12, 13 or 14 digits with a valid check digit mean a GTIN; `http(s)://` with a primary key AI segment means a Digital Link; other data starting with a digit is parsed as an element string and accepted only when it parses completely, its first element is a key and every check passes. Data that is none of these, a leading letter or digits that fail as a GTIN and as an element string (a numeric lot code), is plain data and goes on to steps 4 and 5; `barcode_unknown` comes only after them.
    3. **GS1 keys.** (00) finds a handling unit by SSCC; (01) finds `item_barcode` by GTIN, which gives the item and the unit; (01) with (10) the lot of that item by code; (01) with (21) the serial of that item by code, and with (10) as well, one serial candidate that also carries the lot; (02) with (37) the contained item unit and its count. A lot or serial code the item does not have yet is a candidate without ID, for capture (rule 17). A GTIN without AIs (`]E0`, `]E4`, `]I1`, or 8 to 14 digits) that is not registered is looked up once more as a numeric legacy code, comparing digits padded to 14, so an old internal number printed as EAN-13 or UPC-A still resolves (rule 8). An unknown GTIN is `barcode_unknown` with `details.key: gtin`; the screen offers `CreateItemBarcode`, and an agent may only draft it as a proposal. (414) with (254), (253), (8003) and (8004) are read and reported as `gs1_element_ignored` in Phase 1 (rule 25).
    4. **The registry** for GTINs without AIs, internal codes, legacy codes and other plain data: the row's type leads to the typed row. A reserved row is `barcode_retired`.
    5. **Lots and serials without GS1.** Plain data that is no registered code but is the code of lots or serials of the company resolves only with an item from the context (the task's item, or an item scanned earlier in the step); without one it is `lot_needs_item` or `serial_needs_item`, even when only one item has that code today.
    6. **Context.** In `confirm` mode the expected types and objects filter the candidates; more than one candidate left is `barcode_ambiguous` (a pallet label with (00) and (02) scanned on a search screen, for example).

14. **Typed issues, never a guess.** Issues are check-result codes in the response, each with `details`: `barcode_unknown` (no key, registry row or code matches), `barcode_ambiguous` (several candidates remain; the screen offers a chooser), `barcode_retired` (`details.retired_type`), `lot_needs_item`, `serial_needs_item`, `gs1_invalid` (`details.reason` from rule 3 and `details.ai`), and `scan_malformed`. Information only: `gs1_element_ignored`, `scan_heuristic_used`. A command that registers a legacy code may return `legacy_code_gtin_like` (rule 8). The query never picks the first of several candidates, never strips a check digit that fails, and never reads a lot code without an item.

15. **Scan-to-confirm and scan-to-search.**
    - **Confirm** on every posting step: `ConfirmWarehouseTask` (source bin, handling unit, item, lot, serials, target bin), receipts, deliveries, production issues and receipts, and counts. The step states what it expects: an exact object (the task's source bin), or a type within a scope (a lot of the task's item, a serial of the task's item, an item of the counted bin, any storage bin of the warehouse when the worker changes the target). A scan that resolves to anything else is refused with `scan_mismatch` (`details`: expected and scanned), and a scan with an issue with `scan_unresolved` (`details`: the issues). Nothing is replaced silently.
    - **Overrides** are separate fields of the command with a reason, never a second scan: a different target bin (ADR-0023 rules 12, 15), a shortage reason, typed values when a label is unreadable.
    - **Search** (home screen, stock inquiry, "what is this") calls `EvaluateBarcode` in `search` mode: one candidate opens it, several give a chooser, none offer the unknown-barcode actions. Search only opens records, never posts, and agents never submit scans.

16. **Scans in command payloads.** A scan command carries each scanned field as a `scan` object (`raw`, `symbology_identifier`, `source`, `decoder`, `confirmed_as_read`) next to the field it fills.
    - **The server resolves.** The handler normalizes, parses and resolves `raw` again with the business date and the step's context; the client's own parse is never trusted. The plan and the stored audit payload keep `raw`, so every scan can be replayed and explained.
    - **Untrusted text.** `raw` and every free-text element (lot and serial codes, (400), (90) to (99), the Digital Link domain) come from someone else's label: they carry `is_untrusted` (ADR-0029 rule 10), are validated against their format, and are looked up, never followed.
    - **Idempotency.** Each scan event gets its own idempotency key on the device (ADR-0008 rule 14); the camera de-duplicates the same value for 1.5 seconds before a key is made.

**Capture**

17. **GS1 multi-field capture on receipts.** A scan command's input schema maps AIs to its fields; the mapping is registered with the command (a generated annotation of the JSON Schema), so the PWA, the desktop screen and the server read one table:

    | AI | Field | Rule |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | (01) | `item_id`, `uom_id` | Through `item_barcode`; must equal the line's item when a line is expected (`scan_mismatch`) |
    | (10) | lot | An existing lot of the item by code, or a new lot created by the receipt with that code |
    | (17) | lot `expiry_date` | Set on a new lot; compared on an existing one |
    | (15) | lot `expiry_date` | Only when (17) is absent |
    | (11) | lot `manufacture_date` | As (17) |
    | (21) | serial | An existing serial of the item, or a new one created by the receipt; with (10), in that lot |
    | (30) | `quantity` | In the unit of the (01) barcode |
    | (02) + (37) | `item_id`, `uom_id`, `quantity` | The contained item unit and its count |
    | 310n, 311n, 314n, 315n, 316n | `quantity`, `uom_id` | In the AI's unit (rule 18) |
    | (00) | handling unit | A new handling unit of the receipt takes the SSCC as its barcode in a handling-unit-managed warehouse |

    - **Conflicts stop.** A scanned value that differs from a value the person typed, or from an existing lot's expiry or manufacture date, is refused with `gs1_capture_conflict` (`details`: field, scanned, stored). Lot dates change only by their own command.
    - **Quantity default.** From (30), (37) or a measure AI; otherwise 1 in the barcode's unit (one carton for a carton GTIN). Serial items never default to the task quantity.
    - **Other AIs** ((400), (90) to (99), unmapped measures) stay in the payload's parse result and are reported as `gs1_element_ignored`.
    - **Scope.** Receipts (goods receipts, production receipts, customer returns) in Phase 1, from step 5 on the desktop; picks and packing at step 9 with the PWA.

    Note ([ADR-0063](0063-configurable-policy-settings.md), proposed 2026-11-15): (15) fills the expiry date only while `barcode.gs1.best_before_fills_expiry` reads true for the category; the default false stores it as the lot's `best_before_date` (rule 26).

18. **Variable-measure quantities.** A quantity from a measure AI enters in the AI's unit, which becomes the line's entered unit (a note on ADR-0056 rule 13). It must be an allowed unit of the item (ADR-0056 rule 6), else `uom_not_allowed`. The measure is never rounded: one with more decimals than its unit holds is accepted when the extra decimals are zeros (`3105115600` is 1.15600 kg, entered as 1.156) and otherwise rejected with `too_many_decimal_places` (ADR-0006 rule 14; ADR-0056 rule 8). The line takes the unit block of ADR-0056 rule 9: the snapshot from `convert_quantity`, and the inventory quantity rounded as ADR-0056 rule 8 says for receipts. Examples: `3102001156` on an item kept in `KGM` is 11.56 `KGM`, identity; on an item kept in `GRM` it is 11.56 `KGM` converted through the catalog to 11560 `GRM`; on an item kept in pieces without a kilogram unit it is refused; `3105012345` on any item is refused, since `KGM` holds three decimals.

19. **Serial capture.**
    - **Per unit.** Each unit is one scan; the screen shows a running count ("7 of 20"). A serial scanned twice in one confirmation is refused with `serial_already_scanned`; a serial not in the source bin with an allowed status keeps `serial_not_in_bin` (ADR-0023 rule 14). With (01) and (21), the item of the GTIN must be the task's item (`scan_mismatch`).
    - **By handling unit.** Scanning a closed handling unit whose content is serials of the task's item confirms all its serials at once, when the task takes the whole unit; a partial take unpacks first (ADR-0023 rule 6).
    - **Uniqueness** stays per item (ADR-0020 rule 17). A serial's own internal barcode, when it has one, is unique in the company through the registry.

**Labels**

20. **Label subjects.** Labels are print subjects over registered queries (ADR-0049 rule 25), rendered as PDF by the print worker from company templates:
    - existing: bins (`ListBin`) and handling units (`GetHandlingUnit`);
    - new: `ListItemLabel` (item units, with copies per unit), `ListLotLabel` (lots of an item), `ListSerialLabel` (serials, for receiving and for serials produced at a station, ADR-0034);
    - document prints of barcoded types get the derived field `document.barcode`.
    - Each label row carries `barcode` objects computed by the core: `{symbology, data, human_readable}`. The template only places them, so it cannot build wrong GS1 data. The core ships neutral English templates for each subject (ADR-0049 rule 24).

21. **Symbologies and rendering.**

    | Label | Symbology | Data |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | Bin, document | Code 128 | The internal code or the document number |
    | Handling unit | GS1-128 when its barcode is an SSCC; else Code 128 | (00) SSCC; for a single-item unit also (02), (37) and (10) in a second GS1-128 symbol, since one symbol holds at most 48 data characters |
    | Item unit | GS1 DataMatrix when the unit has a GTIN; else DataMatrix | (01); or the internal code. A template may choose EAN-13 for a GTIN with leading zero for retail points of sale |
    | Lot | GS1 DataMatrix when the item unit has a GTIN and the lot code fits (10); else DataMatrix | (01) (10), with (17) when the lot has an expiry date; or the lot's internal barcode |
    | Serial | GS1 DataMatrix when the item unit has a GTIN and the serial code fits (21); else DataMatrix | (01) (21); or the serial's internal barcode |

    - **Encoded data is ASCII.** GS1 strings are built and validated by `erpme.gs1` before rendering, with FNC1 in the first position, never parentheses, fixed-length elements first and one variable element last where the AIs allow it. The human-readable line may carry text in any language in the print language, with the fonts the worker ships (ADR-0049 rule 24).
    - **Vector bars.** The worker draws barcodes as SVG with Zint, called by the core's template filter `barcode`. It runs Zint's command-line program inside the worker's sandbox (GS1 mode, SVG to standard output), so no Python binding is needed; `erpme.gs1` has already validated the data, and Zint's own GS1 check, whose AI table may lag the pinned Dictionary, is switched off. The options are verified at build. Licences: libzint is BSD-3-Clause and the Zint command-line program GPL-3.0 (GPL-3.0-or-later if the build check confirms it); running it as a separate program is aggregation, and both are compatible with the core's AGPL-3.0 (spec 18; to verify at build). Whoever distributes the worker image ships Zint's licence texts with it and offers the corresponding source of the command-line program, as the GPL requires. The filter is the only core filter whose output is markup: the markup is geometry the core generates from validated data, the human-readable text is escaped, and templates still cannot emit markup of their own (a note on ADR-0049 rule 24).
    - **Print quality.** Templates state module size and quiet zones; the renderer refuses values below the symbology's minimum. For thermal printers a template may state the printer's resolution, and modules are then whole printer dots.

**PWA capture**

22. **Capture in the PWA and on desktop screens.**
    - **Camera.** Until the scanner check page proves on a device that BarcodeDetector passes FNC1 and GS through (unverified; open question), BarcodeDetector serves only the formats that need no identifier: EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A, UPC-E and ITF. Code 128, GS1-128, DataMatrix and QR go through a WebAssembly decoder based on ZXing-C++ (Apache-2.0), which reports the symbology identifier and FNC1 (`decoder: wasm`); it also serves every format where BarcodeDetector is missing (iOS, desktop Linux). The decoder and the GS1 Syntax Engine load on first use, outside ADR-0040's JavaScript budget, and their size is measured in DS-31. Camera UX: a target box, torch, the same value de-duplicated for 1.5 seconds, haptic and sound feedback with a distinct error tone, a tap to choose among several codes in view, and never an automatic submit while several codes are visible unless exactly one matches the expected type.
    - **Without an identifier** from BarcodeDetector, the PWA sends `camera_format` (`ean_13`, `itf`, …), which stands in for the identifier; a leading or embedded GS marks GS1 data as in rule 13 step 2.
    - **Keyboard wedge.** Scan screens keep one focused input with `inputmode="none"`. A scan is recognized by the AIM prefix `]`, the Enter suffix and timing (characters faster than a person types), and its `source` is `wedge`; slower input is `manual`. Every code field on desktop screens accepts wedge scans too and resolves them with the field's expected type (a note on ADR-0040 rule 16).

23. **Scanner profiles and the check page.**
    - **Profiles.** The core ships configuration for the supported rugged scanner families (a Zebra DataWedge profile, Honeywell and Datalogic setting codes): AIM symbology identifier on, GS replaced by `~`, Enter suffix, and the symbologies Code 128, GS1-128, DataMatrix, GS1 DataMatrix, QR, EAN/UPC and ITF on. DS-31 keeps a short list of tested devices.
    - **Check page.** The PWA's scanner check page shows what arrives for each code of a GS1 test sheet the core ships as a PDF: the raw characters with control characters made visible, the identifier, the parsed elements and the resolution. It compares each code with the text the sheet encodes, so it detects a lost GS or identifier, a flipped case, and characters changed by a keyboard layout or an input method editor, and says what to change. It also records whether BarcodeDetector on that device passes GS through (rule 22).

**Seams, later and build**

24. **Traceability-code hook.** The core keeps GS1 keys, lots, serials and labels generic and depends on no country's rules.
    - **The hook.** `TraceabilityCodeProvider` is a published package interface (ADR-0016 rule 17): `traceability_code(item, lot or serial, location, business date) -> {code, label_fields} | None`. When the company's package implements it, label subjects add the optional field `traceability`; when none does, nothing changes. Phase 1 ships no implementation.
    - **Vietnam, flagged.** The 2026-11-15 survey reports that Vietnam's Decree 37/2026/NĐ-CP defines a traceability code and a digital product passport, with traceability mandatory for goods on a list still to be published (legal portals only, unverified against the official gazette). Any such output is `l10n_vn` package code behind this hook, with its basis `unverified` (ADR-0016 rule 6; ADR-0053), never core. Open question (DS-40).

25. **After Phase 1.**
    - ZPL templates as a second render target and a local print agent or socket printing for thermal printers.
    - GS1 location and asset keys: (414) with (254) for bins, GRAI (8003), GIAI (8004), GDTI (253).
    - Digital Link generation (QR on consumer units), with a resolver; compressed Digital Link.
    - An offline scanning queue: idempotent commands queued on the device, validated against claimed-task snapshots and a registry subset, conflicts returned by the server (ADR-0023 rule 22).
    - SSCC assignment (extension digit, the generation prefix, a serial reference from a counter that never repeats) when a customer requires SSCC labels, earlier if a pilot needs it.
    - Decoding of restricted circulation numbers with an embedded weight or price; until then they resolve like any registered GTIN.
    - Serial range entry with a right, serial uniqueness per company as a product setting, and component-to-parent serial genealogy.

26. **Build placement** (proposed; the runbook and spec 11 are the author's): the registry, the trigger and the commands with master data at step 2; `erpme.gs1`, `EvaluateBarcode` and scan-to-confirm in the warehouse core at step 3; desktop capture on receipts and labels with the print worker at step 5; camera, wedge capture, profiles and the check page with the PWA at step 9.

## Context

The design already treated barcodes as a lookup key and scans as commands (ADR-0018 rule 14, ADR-0023). What it lacked is what every warehouse system ends up needing once suppliers' labels arrive: a parser for GS1 element strings, check digits, a way to tell what a scanned string is, and rules for labels.

**What the survey found.** The 2026-11-15 survey compared the large systems:
- SAP EWM decodes GS1-128 into fields and checks every scan against the expected bin, handling unit or product: its verification fields are the model of scan-to-confirm.
- Dynamics 365 maps AIs to the fields of each mobile step and needs the scanner to send an identifier and a GS substitute.
- Odoo's nomenclature of ordered rules lets a cross-type collision surface only at scan time.
- NetSuite fills lot, serial and quantity from one scan.
- Business Central has no standard multi-AI parser.

Every vendor ships the AI table and depends on scanner setup. ERPMe generates the table from GS1's own Dictionary, keeps the AI-to-field mapping in its command registry, and ships scanner profiles and a check page.

**Determinism.** A scan is a string; code parses and resolves it, as code computes every number (Principle 13). The query returns candidates and typed issues, so an agent or a screen never has to guess, and a posting step accepts only what it expects.

**Global first.** GS1 is used in every market ERPMe targets. Companies without a GS1 prefix, common among small manufacturers, work on internal codes. Country rules, such as a national traceability code, sit behind a package hook, as ADR-0053 requires.

## Options considered

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typed tables plus a shared uniqueness table filled by a trigger (chosen) | Keeps `item_barcode` with its unit and ADR-0056's key, bins and handling units unchanged; the database holds cross-type uniqueness; one lookup | A trigger writes a table; retired codes accumulate |
| One `barcode` table replacing the typed barcodes | One place for every code | Rewrites three accepted ADRs; loses the unit binding of `item_barcode` |
| A view over the typed tables, uniqueness by commands | No new table | Collisions are found only by commands and invariants, as in Odoo |
| Foreign keys from each typed table to the registry, rows written by commands | No trigger | Every command and fixture must write two rows in order; a forgotten registration is a failed command instead of a guarantee |
| A padded-digits test inside the trigger (round 1) | No extra index | Not held under concurrent inserts; the unique expression index is |
| A hand-made AI table | Simple | Every vendor's bugs come from it: wrong fixed-length flags, missing pairs |
| Parsing only in the PWA (WebAssembly) | Fast feedback | Desktop screens, imports and agents need the same answer; the server must decide |
| PDF labels in Phase 1, ZPL later (chosen) | Fits the print engine; any printer | Slower on thermal printers |
| ZPL in Phase 1 | Exact bars, speed | A second template language and a print agent before the volume needs them |
| Case-sensitive internal codes | Code 128 can carry lower case | Wedges and keyboard layouts flip case; two codes that differ only by case confuse people |
| Refusing legacy numeric codes that are not GTINs | Keeps one internal format | Breaks migration: labels on racks and goods would all have to be replaced at cut-over (ADR-0044) |
| GS1 character set 82 on every lot and serial code | Every code fits (10) and (21) | Refuses old and supplier lot numbers; the lot's own barcode covers them |
| `ResolveBarcode` with a new query verb | Reads naturally | Gives `Resolve` a second meaning against ADR-0004 rule 20; `Evaluate` already is the form |

## Constraints that come with the decision

- Scanners must be configured: AIM identifier on and a GS substitute. The profiles and the check page make that a setup step, not a support case; without an identifier, the guarded rules apply and are reported.
- The pinned Dictionary needs an update pull request when GS1 publishes a release.
- Retired codes stay reserved until their owner takes them back or a person deletes the reservation; the registry grows with every relabel.
- Every bin, handling unit, item barcode, lot or serial barcode must be stored in normalized form; fixtures and migrations follow.
- Legacy codes keep old labels readable but block any supplier GTIN with the same digits; `legacy_code_gtin_like` points them out.
- A barcoded document type's number format must start with its reserved prefix and produce internal codes only.
- The PWA needs the WebAssembly decoder for Code 128, GS1-128, DataMatrix and QR until devices prove BarcodeDetector passes GS through.
- The print worker gains Zint's command-line program; the PWA gains two WebAssembly modules loaded on demand.

## Revisit when

- A customer requires SSCC labels, ZPL printing or offline scanning in Phase 1.
- GS1 changes the predefined-length table or the Digital Link syntax in a way the generator rejects.
- The check page shows that BarcodeDetector passes FNC1 and GS through on the supported devices.
- A country's traceability rules become mandatory for goods a pilot sells.
- Restricted circulation numbers with embedded weights are needed.
